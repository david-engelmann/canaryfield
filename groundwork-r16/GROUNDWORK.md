## Subject 1: the two repr copies

The duplication is exactly two things, stated narrowly so the slice can be too: the printable reading (which codepoints the Python repr leaves unescaped) exists twice, and the string spelling built on it exists twice.

Copy A stands in src/json.rs and serves the seed pack surface. py_repr (line 534) renders a JsonValue the way the Python repr renders it, containers and scalars included, and its string arm calls py_str_repr (line 556), which escapes through a private is_printable (line 601) reading a static table, NON_PRINTABLE_RANGES (lines 621 to 1334, 714 raw lines of the file's 1,387). The table holds 712 merged non-printable ranges. Its comment states its provenance: generated from the unicodedata of CPython 3.12 over Unicode 15.0.0 by a generator kept in the Round 14 scratch. That generator stands nowhere in this tree.

Copy B stands in src/pydata.rs and src/pyunparse.rs and serves the tree surface. pydata::is_printable (pydata.rs line 65) reads src/printable_ranges.txt (711 lines, one printable range per line) through the ranges loader of pydata.rs, and pyunparse::str_repr (pyunparse.rs line 1468) spells a text constant from its codepoints, testing each through is_printable_cp (line 1402), which delegates to pydata::is_printable for any codepoint that stands as a char and answers false for the rest. pydata.rs's header says both its tables were generated by tools/gen_unicode_names.py, but that script as landed writes only src/unicode_names.txt and src/unified_ranges.txt, so the printable table's generator stands nowhere in this tree either.

Consumers, every call site found by search and read:

- Copy A: src/seedpack.rs lines 214, 222, and 233, the loader refusals that carry the repr of an entry (missing fields, a field holding no text, the whole entry). Nothing else calls py_repr outside src/json.rs's own unit tests and the direct pins at tests/port1_review_fixes.rs lines 98 and 109.
- Copy B: src/pydump.rs lines 167 and 198 spell every string constant a dump carries, and src/pyunparse.rs lines 1067 and 1113 spell string text inside the unparse renderings, with str_literal_helper falling back to str_repr at line 1435.

Recordings that pin each copy today:

- Copy A is pinned by the PORT1 battery, tests/conformance/port1_cases.json, 57 cases of which 56 are load cases replayed by tests/conformance.rs with refusal texts compared word for word (its census assertion stands at 56), and by the json.rs unit tests and port1_review_fixes pins named above.
- Copy B is pinned by the PORT2 battery, tests/conformance/port2_cases.json, 927 cases (910 apply, 17 plant) replayed by tests/port2_conformance.rs, where every recorded dump and render spells its string constants through copy B, and by the pins in tests/port2_cr_fixes.rs and tests/port2_review_fixes.rs.

Whether the answers can diverge today: by the tables, no, and by behavior, no, both proven rather than read. probe_tables.py computed both printable sets over all 1,114,112 codepoints: copy A against copy B differ at 0 codepoints, and each against the interpreter's own str.isprintable reading differs at 0 codepoints. probe_repr.rs and probe_repr_expected.py then spelled a seam set through both copies and through the interpreter (22 single codepoints across the control, format, separator, private use, and unassigned categories, plus quote mixes, backslash, and the escape whitespace shapes), and all three spellings agree on every line of the recorded outputs. The hazard the debt names is therefore not a present divergence but a standing one: two tables that must be edited together forever, one of them 714 lines of a 1,387 line file, and neither regenerable from anything in the tree, so the first Unicode refresh or one-sided fix silently forks the readings.

The one place the copies differ in kind is the lone surrogate. Copy B's input is a slice of codepoints, so it carries U+D800 and spells it as the interpreter does (probe: str_repr over [0xD800] gives the escape spelling, and over a mixed text likewise, matching repr of the same Python string). Copy A's input is a Rust text string, which cannot hold a lone surrogate at all, so no input the JSON path can hand it ever presents one, and the pack loader's own contract text (PORT-4) already refuses a surrogate escape at the decoder. The seam is structural, not behavioral: copy A has no route to that codepoint, and any future surface that must spell one through the pack path would have to grow one.

What unification onto copy B touches: src/json.rs loses the static table (714 raw lines), its private is_printable, and the escape loop of py_str_repr, delegating the string arm of py_repr to pyunparse::str_repr over the text's codepoints and keeping only the container and scalar arms (None, True, False, the integer text, and float_text, which is the JSON module's own machinery and no part of either repr copy). src/seedpack.rs keeps calling json::py_repr at the same three sites with the same signature. No recorded answer on either battery can move, on the probe evidence above: the tables are the same set, the spellings agree on every seam probed, and the PORT1 and PORT2 replays compare those answers word for word and byte for byte. The surviving table also needs what neither copy has today, a generator in tools/ that writes src/printable_ranges.txt from the running interpreter, on the pattern of tools/gen_xid_tables.py.

## Subject 2: report.py, the unpinned module

canaryfield/report.py stands at 61 raw lines, 54 by the counting method. Its whole public surface is two functions taking one result dict, to_json and to_text. Its only consumer in the package is canaryfield/cli.py, which writes both renderings to the out directory (lines 81 and 82) and prints one of them (line 83). The result dicts it renders are built by engine.run_field in both planting modes with the same keys. No pin on either function stands in either suite: a search of tests/ for the module, its functions, and its output phrases returns only the engine bridge tests, which pin the result dict's contents and never render it, and the header of tests/test_seedpack.py, which records where the report pins went. The two rendered specimens in examples/ (catch-table.txt and catch-table.json, and the pair under examples/two-gates/) are compared by no test.

The format, from probe_report.py run against the landed module (its full outputs are banked):

- The standing specimen round-trips exactly: loading examples/catch-table.json as a result, to_text reproduces examples/catch-table.txt byte for byte and to_json reproduces the JSON file byte for byte. The specimens are true renderings of the current code over that one result.
- to_json is json.dumps with indent 2 and sorted keys, plus one trailing newline.
- to_text opens with the two title lines and a blank, then the target line and the target unchanged line (the boolean spelled lower case, true or false), then one clean exit line per gate in the order the result lists the gates, then a blank, then the table: the header seed, name, planted file, then one column per gate in the same order. Each column's width is the widest cell or header in it, cells join with two spaces, padded and right stripped, and a rule line of dashes follows the header. A row whose planted file is missing spells none. A row carrying more than one planted file adds an indented planted in line naming them joined by commas. Exactly one planted file adds no line, and a row with no planted_files key renders the same (that read goes through a get with an empty default, as does the unparseable list). A non-empty unparseable list adds a blank and the files skipped as unparseable line. The report closes with a blank, the summary per gate line, and one line per gate giving caught, waved-through, not-applicable, and gate-broken counts in that spelling and order, and the whole text ends with one trailing newline.
- The crash family, which a pin set must state as refusals of a future port rather than imitate: an empty rows list raises TypeError at the width computation (max over an empty star of row widths), and a gate missing from summary, from clean_exits, from a row's gate cells, or the gates key itself missing, each raises KeyError naming the gate. The engine never builds an empty rows list (it iterates the 48 registry seeds), so the empty shape is reachable only by a hand-built result, but the module accepts a plain dict and the shape is its honest edge.

A pin set that can actually fail must assert, at minimum: the specimen round-trip both ways (which fails the day any spelling above moves), the width rule on a result whose widest cell stands in a row rather than the header, the planted in line at two planted files and its absence at one, the none spelling, the unparseable block present and absent, gate order taken from the result and never sorted, the summary spellings and counts, the lower case boolean both ways, the trailing newlines of both renderings, and the crash family stated as the module's own refusals if and when it is ported. The retired pins this restores stood in tests/test_slice6.py and tests/test_round1_fixes.py and were removed by the PORT2-9 retirement in the same files the pack pins were restored from.

Port candidacy: the module is a pure rendering of a plain dict at 54 counted lines, the smallest unported surface in the package, and the repo's own record has already assigned its pins to its port: the test_seedpack.py header states the report pins were not restored because they belong to the slice that ports report.py, and PORT2-9 names the report module among the boundary the following slices take. The recommendation below therefore treats report.py as a port candidate for its own slice, not as a pinning subject for this one: pinning it in Python now would rebuild exactly the suite the port slice retires, and its consumer is the command line, whose slice the bridge pattern of Round 15 (engine.plant_seed over the crate binary) already shows how to serve.

## Subject 3: seed-17, proven at plant level only

seed-17 is the swapped dictionary entry of obligation K-3: at every dictionary literal carrying at least one keyed entry it exchanges the key and the value of every keyed entry, leaving unpacking entries standing. Its registry record stands at src/registry.rs line 133 and in canaryfield/seed-pack.json, name swapped dictionary entry, defect class wrong data.

The discrimination the review record names is a shape where the exchange changes nothing: over a dictionary whose key and value are the same expression, the apply returns True (a match was collected and planted) while the dump of the tree after the call equals the dump of the freshly parsed source, so the bool and the planting are different answers, and the dump test in plant_seed, not the bool, decides what counts as planted. The PORT2 preamble records this as a probed finding of the contract itself, over {key: key}.

What stands today, all of it verified against the landed battery (tests/conformance/port2_cases.json):

- At plant level the proof stands as its own case, plant:dict-nochange-17, whose only file reads d = {key: key} and whose recorded seed-17 answer is planted standing empty, skipped standing empty, and the after bytes of the file identical to its source bytes. seed-09 plants that same file in the same case, so the case separates the two readings on the same input.
- At apply level no case isolates it. 869 of the 910 apply cases carry a seed-17 answer. 24 of them record a first application bool of True, and in all 24 the recorded dump differs from the case's standing dump, which a mechanical scan of the battery confirms. No apply case carries a source holding a self-mapping dictionary entry.
- The battery's recording convention already holds the shape the missing case needs: where an application leaves the dump and the render standing, the record carries the bool alone with no dump or render key beside it (20 first applications stand in that shape today, against 2,028 that carry a changed dump), and tests/port2_conformance.rs replays a missing key as the standing answer.
- The port's own behavior over the shape executes today (probe_seed17.rs): over d = {key: key} and d = {1: 1} the port's apply answers True with dump and render both unchanged, and a second application answers True again. Over an ordinary dictionary it answers True with the dump changed. Over an empty dictionary and an unpacking-only dictionary it answers False.

A suite-level proof therefore requires exactly one thing: a synthetic corpus case added to the generator in tools/gen_port2_recordings.py whose source holds a self-mapping dictionary (the plant case's own d = {key: key} serves), regenerated into the battery as an addition, where seed-17's recorded first application is the bool True standing alone and its second application likewise. No test code changes: the standing replay compares the bool and, finding no dump key, asserts the dump stands. The contract can oblige that case by name, or decline it by name. What it cannot honestly do is leave the discrimination claimed on the plant case alone, which is the state the Round 15 review recorded.

## The generators

The standing pattern holds in this repo: tools/ carries all three generators in the tree, tools/gen_port2_recordings.py (506 counted lines, the PORT2 battery's maker), tools/gen_unicode_names.py (the name and unified range tables), and tools/gen_xid_tables.py (the identifier tables). The two exceptions in the whole tree are the two repr tables of Subject 1, one attributed to a scratch generator that no longer stands and one attributed by its module header to a script that does not write it.

## Slicing recommendation

One round, three dispositions.

IN, the repr unification: delete copy A out of src/json.rs (the static table, the private is_printable, the escape loop of py_str_repr) and spell the pack path's strings through copy B, keeping py_repr's container and scalar arms in place. Add the missing generator for src/printable_ranges.txt under tools/ and correct the pydata.rs header's provenance sentence to name it. Proof is the standing proof: both batteries replay unchanged, and the slice adds a seam pin set over the unified path (the surrogate through the tree surface, the separator and format codepoints through both surfaces) so the reading stands pinned in one place. No recorded answer moves, and the battery files do not regenerate.

IN, riding the same generator run, the seed-17 apply-level case: one synthetic corpus case in tools/gen_port2_recordings.py over a self-mapping dictionary, the battery regenerated as additions only (the new case beside the 927), its closing census assertion moving by the count the slice states. This is the whole of the seed-17 debt. The plant-level proof already stands and needs nothing.

OUT, report.py whole: no port, no pins in this round. Its disposition is its own slice next, port and pins together, exactly as the test_seedpack.py header and PORT2-9's boundary already assign it, with the format census and the crash family above standing as that slice's groundwork of record. The engine, the command line, the registry, and every other module stand untouched, and B-1's unification here sets the pattern the ostensible line's identical debt can take when its own slice comes.

The census supports the round as drawn: the unification is a deletion with a proven-equal replacement, the seed-17 case is one generator line of corpus, and nothing in either touches a recorded answer.
